Note: D6 and D are used to mean a 6 sided dice interchangeably. 2D6 = 2D
The term world creation is a bit of a misnomer. We instead create a planetary system. During World creation we flesh out the details of the single most important and most travelled world in a star system; additional planets are assumed to exist but are left to the discretion of the games referee.

## World Profiles
Traveller describes planets using a single line of hexadecimal code. Hexadecimal codes use base 16 notation; digits 0 through 9 are represented by common numbers; digits 10 through 15 are represented by the letters A through F. A is equal to 10, B equal to 11 and so on - The highest digit will be F, equal to 15. In some unusual cases, further sequences of letters can also be used, such as using G as a digit to describe Tech Level (TL) 16.

This is an example world: 'COGRI 0101 CA6A643-9 N RI WA A'
The first component is the name: 'COGRI'
The second component is a four digit number giving it's hex location on a sector map: '0101'
The next string of digits denote, in order:
Startport Quality: 'C'
Size: 'A'
Atmosphere type: '6'
Hydrographic percentage: 'A'
Population: '6'
Government Type: '4'
Law Level: '3'
Tech Level: '9'

The next component marks any bases present on the world - examples include N for Naval Base and S for Scout Base: 'N'
This if followed by any Trade codes for the planet. Cogri is a rich waterworld: 'RI WA'
The travel zone for the system is last; A=Amber Zone, R=Red Zone. If no code is given then the world is either unclassified or a Green Zone: 'A'

# Steps to world creation

The basic planetary codes are size, atmosphere, hydrographics, population, government, law level, technology level, starport and bases, and are generated using 2D each, with various Dice Modifiers (DM) applied based upon the results of other codes.
When generating a planet run through the below sections in order.

## Gas Giants
A star system may have one or more gas giant planets (similar to jupiter or saturn). The presence of a gas giant allows streamlined ships to refuel by skimming their atmosphere; this eliminates fuel cost for the vessel and increases profit, as well as refuelling at systems that do not have a star port. To determine is a system has a gas giant roll 2D6. On a 10 or higher there is NO gas giant (i.e. only if you roll a 10, 11 or 12). Gas giants are common so most of the time they are present. 

## Size
The size code for worlds ranges from 0 to 10, and is determined by rolling 2D6-2 (adding the results of two 6 sided dice and subtracting 2 from the total). Size measures a world diameter in thousands of kilometers and the bigger a planet, the higher it's gravity.

## Atmosphere
The atmosphere code measures the type of atmosphere present. A planet's atmosphere is generated by rolling 2D-7 and adding the planet's size. For instance If I rolled a 2 and a 3 I'd have a sum of 5 on my two dice, subtract 7 and it's -2, then once I add my planet size of A (10) I have an atmosphere of 8. Any values below 0 or above F (15) are clipped.

## Temperature
Although not mentioned in the profile string it's an important early step to get the temp. Temperature is determined by rolling 2D and applying the following DMs listed below:
Atmosphere 2 or 3: DM-2
Atmosphere 4, 5 or E (14): DM-1
Atmosphere 8 or 9: DM+1
Atmosphere A (10), D (13) or F (15): DM+2
Atmosphere B (11) or C (12): DM+6

| Temperature | Type | Average Temperature | Description |
|:---:|---|---|---|
| 2 or less | Frozen | -51 or less | Frozen world. No liquid water, very dry atmosphere. |
| 3 to 4 | Cold | -51 to 0 | Icy World. Little liquid water, extensive ice caps, few clouds. |
| 5 to 9 | Temperate | 0 to 30 | Temperate world. Earth-like. Liquid & vaporised water are common, moderate ice caps |
| 10 to 11 | Hot | 31 to 80 | Hot World. Small or no ice caps, little liquid water. Most water in the form of clouds. |
| 12 or more | Boiling | 81+ | Boiling world. No ice caps, little liquid water. |

## Hydrographics
The hydrographic code measures the amount of surface liquid and hence the amount of dry land on the world. Hydrographic percentage goes from 0 to A (10), measuring in increments of 10%. For normal worlds this will be water, for other worlds with corrosive or exotic atmospheres it could be corrosive or exotic liquids like ammonia or methane. It's possible to have a waterworld planet covered entirely in liquid methane.

Hydrographic code is obtained by rolling 2D-7 and adding the planet's Atmosphere. Also apply the following DMs:
If the planet's size is 0 or 1, then Hydrographic is always 0. 
If the Atmosphere is 0, 1 or A, B, C, D, E, F then subtract 4.
If the atmosphere is **not** D (13) or F (15) and then apply the following temperature based DMs: Hot (temp 10-11) DM-2, Boiling (temp 12+) DM-6

i.e. A planet of atmosphere C (12) might roll a 10 on two dice, subtract 7 to get 3, add 12 then subtract another 4 due to atmosphere type, to get 11. This is clipped down to 10, meaning 100% hydrographic, a waterworld.

| 0 | 0%-5% | Desert world |
| 1 | 6%-15% | Dry world |
| 2 | 16%-25% | A few small seas |
| 3 | 26%-35% | Small seas and oceans |
| 4 | 36%-45% | Wet world |
| 5 | 46%-55% | A large ocean |
| 6 | 56%-65% | Large oceans |
| 7 | 66%-75% | Earth-like world |
| 8 | 76%-85% | Only a few islands and archipelagos |
| 9 | 86%-95% | Almost entirely water |

## Population
The population measures the planet's intelligent population. The population code can be roughly generalised as the number of zeroes following a one, so population 6 indicates a population in the millions (1,000,000).
Population is generated with 2D-2. 
A world with a population 1 can change its government code with an argument over dinner and is unlikely to bother with a worldwide network of communications satellites even at tech level 15.
If a world has population 0, it is uninhabited and so has a government, law level and Technology level of 0.

## Rival factions
The government code determines the dominant government type on that planet but there are usually other factions such as rival political parties, cultural groups, religions, rebels and so forth who oppose the government.
Roll D3 (three sided dice) to determine how many factions there are on the planet, with DM+1 if the government is 0 or 7 (and not uninhabited) and DM-1 if the government is 10 or more. 
For each faction use the government table again to determine what form it takes. Then roll 2D on the factions table below to determine the strength of each faction.

| Strength | Relative Strength |
|---|---|
| 2 to 3 | Obscure group - few have heard of them, no popular support |
| 4 to 5 | Fringe group - few supporters |
| 6 to 7 | Minor group - some supporters |
| 8 to 9 | Notable group - significant support, well known |
| 10 to 11 | Significant - nearly as powerful as the government |
| 12 | Overwhelming popular support - more powerful than the government |

## Law level
The law level of a planet represents the relative force of law extant on the world. A high law level indicates that visitors to the world will have their activities curtailed. Law level determines two things in particular - what it is illegal for Travellers to possess on the world and the likelihood the Travellers will run afoul of the law.
Law level is determined by rolling 2D-7 and adding the government code

## Starport
Starports are classed from A to E. To determine the level of a starport on a planet, roll 2D and apply the following DMs:
If the planet's population is 8 to 9: DM+1
If the planet's population is 10 or more: DM+2
If the planet's population is 3 to 4: DM-1
If the planet's population is 2 or less: DM-2

Note for the hexcode we don't list the dice result, but instead the Class.

| Dice result + DM | Class | Quality | Weekly berthing cost | Fuel | Facilities | Bases |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 or less | X | No starport | 0 | None | None | Corsair 10+ |
| 3 to 4 | E | Frontier | 0 | None | None | Corsair 10+ |
| 5 to 6 | D | Poor | 1D x Cr10 | Unrefined | Limited Repair, Highport 12+ | Scout 8+, Corsair 12+ |
| 7 to 8 | C | Routine | 1D x Cr100 | Unrefined | Shipyard (small craft), repair, Highport 10+ | Military 10+, scout 9+ |
| 9 to 10 | B | Good | 1D x Cr500 | Refined | Shipyard (spacecraft), repair, Highport 8+ | Military 8+, Naval 8+, Scout 9+ |
| 11+ | A | Excellent | 1D x Cr1000 | Refined | Shipyard (all), repair, Highport 6+ | Military 8+, naval 8+, scout 10+ |

The table above contains several checks for whether a highport is present. To roll for a highport, roll 2D and add the following modifiers:
DM+1 if Tech Level 9-11
DM+2 if Tech Level 12+
DM+1 if population 9+
DM-1 if population 6 or less
If the result is equal to or higher than the one listed (6+ for class A, 8+ for class B etc) then a starport is present.

Similarly, a corsair (C) base may be present in the system. Roll 2D and add the following modifiers:
DM+2 if Law Level 0
DM-2 is Law Level 2 or higher
For starports X, E and D there is a listed check for a corsair base, either 10+ or 12+

Finally, for the Military (M), scout (S) and naval (N) bases there are no DMs, just roll 2D and compare against the listed checks.

Note that due to the slightly circular nature of starbases and tech level, you will have to first determine the class of the starport, then determine the tech level, and finally come back to check the bases and highport.

## Trade codes
Trade codes designate particular types of worlds and the types of goods likely to be found there. If a world meets all requirements listed for one or more Trade codes it gains those classifications.

Agricultural (Ag): Dedicated o farming and food production.
Asteroids (As): Usually mining colonies but can also be orbital factories or colonies.
Barren (Ba): Uncolonised and empty.
Desert (De): Dry and barely habitable.
Fluid Oceans (Fl): Worlds where the surface liquid is something other than water and so are incompatible with earth-derived life.
Garden (Ga): Worlds that are Earth-like.
High Population (Hi): A population in the billions.
High Tech (Ht): Among the most technologically advanced in Chartered space.
Ice-capped (Ic): Worlds that have most of their surface liquid frozen in polar ice caps and are cold and dry.
Industrial (In): Dominated by factories and cities.
Low Population (Lo): A population of only a few thousand or less.
Low Tech (Lt): Pre-industrial and cannot produce advanced goods.
Non-Agricultural (Na): Too dry or barren to support their populaiton using conventional food production.
Non-Industrial (Ni): Too low in population to maintain an extensive industrial base.
Poor (Po): Lacking resources, viable land or sufficient population to be anything other than marginal colonies.
Rich (Ri): Blessed with a stable governemnt and viable biosphere, making them economic powerhouses.
Vacuum (Va): No atmosphere
Water World (Wa): Almost entirely water-ocean across their surface.

## Travel codes
The two travel codes are red and amber. Red is only given at the referee's discretion. A system is Amber if the world has atmosphere 10+, a government 0, 7 or 10, or a Law Level of 0 or 9+.
